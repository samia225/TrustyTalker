# TrustyTalker: project summary

**Problem.** JARA is MCRC Health's research-stage chatbot for adults with substance use and mental health concerns. Some replies sound supportive but miss what the person needs. A reply might encourage someone to quit when they only asked for information, overlook an earlier preference, state something its sources do not support, or miss signs of risk. Reviewers need a dependable way to find these replies.

**Approach.** TrustyTalker is a review tool that runs offline in any web browser. It follows each person's goal, readiness and preferences through the conversation and applies 16 rule-based checks to JARA's replies, covering goal alignment, drift, source support, crisis response, unsafe advice and more. Each finding quotes the reply, rates its severity as critical or quality, and suggests where the fix belongs. An optional AI judge looks for subtler problems, and a human reviewer makes the final decision.

**Results.** We created 28 fictional cases (59 conversations), including three scenarios written and rated by MCRC, and labelled them before running the checks. A second team member independently agreed with every label they checked, and MCRC reviewed the labels. The checks agree with our answer key on 57 of 59 conversations (97%). They identify 42 of 44 problem replies, including all 17 critical ones, with no false alarms. The two misses were mildly pushy replies. These are development results, since the team saw every case. On seven longer cases the rules were never tuned on, they catch 11 of 14 problems. Clinician review is the next step.
