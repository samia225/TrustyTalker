# TrustyTalker: project summary

**Problem.** JARA, MCRC Health's research-stage support chatbot for substance use and mental health, can give replies that sound kind but ignore the person: pushing someone to quit when they only wanted information, forgetting an earlier preference, stating facts its sources do not support, or missing signs of risk. Reviewers need a fast, reliable way to find these replies.

**Approach.** TrustyTalker is an offline review tool that opens in any browser. It tracks each person's goal, readiness and preferences turn by turn, then runs 12 rule-based checks on JARA's replies, including goal alignment, source support, crisis response, unsafe advice, scope and respectful language. Each suggestion quotes the exact words, gives a severity (critical or quality) and says where to fix it (prompt, safety rule, retrieval or model). A human reviewer confirms or rejects every suggestion. Severity rules were agreed with MCRC.

**Results.** We wrote 25 fictional cases (50 conversations) and labelled them before running the checks. A second teammate labelled a sample independently and agreed on all 12 conversations. The checks match our answer key on 46 of 50 conversations (92%). They caught 30 of 35 problem replies, all 11 critical ones, with one false alarm across 27 clean conversations. Source support, goal alignment and crisis response caught every labelled problem. These are development numbers: the team could see every case while writing rules, and there is no hidden test set. Known gaps: the drift and saved-state checks are not built yet. Labels come from our team, not clinicians.
